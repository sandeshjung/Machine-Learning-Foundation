Optimiser race: GD, momentum, RMSProp and Adam

All four optimisers start from the same point and only see the local slope (the gradient). Pick a surface, press play, and compare the paths they take to the bottom.

What to try

  1. Narrow bowl. The surface is ten times steeper in one direction than the other. Plain gradient descent zig-zags across the steep walls. Momentum builds up speed along the valley floor, and Adam rescales each direction so it heads more directly to the minimum.
  2. Too large a step. On the bowl, raise the GD learning rate above 0.2. The zig-zag grows instead of shrinking: it diverges.
  3. Rosenbrock banana. Finding the curved valley is easy. Following it to the minimum at (1, 1) is hard. Plain GD needs a tiny learning rate and crawls. Momentum and Adam get much further in the same number of steps.
  4. Two valleys. Start on the right-hand side. GD settles in the nearer, local minimum. With enough momentum, the optimiser can roll over the hump into the deeper, global one. Try μ = 0.5 vs 0.95.
  5. Move the start. Click different starting points. On a non-convex surface, where you start decides where you end.

What's going on

Each optimiser turns the gradient g = ∇f(θ) into a step, using the PyTorch update rules:

  • Gradient descent: θ ← θ − η·g. The step is proportional to the slope, so it's large on steep walls and tiny on flat floors.
  • Momentum: v ← μ·v + g, then θ ← θ − η·v. Consistent gradients add up and zig-zags cancel out, like a heavy ball rolling downhill.
  • RMSProp: divides each coordinate's gradient by a running average of its size, so steep and flat directions take similar-sized steps.
  • Adam: momentum plus RMSProp-style scaling, with bias correction for the first few steps. Its steps are roughly η in size, whatever the scale of the gradient.
The two learning-rate sliders are separate because the methods use η differently. For GD and momentum, the step is η × gradient. For RMSProp and Adam, the step is about η, regardless of how steep the surface is. These update rules are checked against torch.optim in docs/tests.
